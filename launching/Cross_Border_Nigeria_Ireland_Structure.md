## Should You Channel Ideas Through the Nigerian Atensai Entity?

### Short Answer: Not for the primary tech products.

### Why Irish Entity is Better for Tech Apps

1. **Legal credibility in EU/UK/US markets** — users and partners trust an EU-registered entity more than a Nigerian one for consumer apps (unfortunate reality of international perception)
2. **App Store requirements** — Apple and Google developer accounts registered to an Irish company give you cleaner EU merchant-of-record status
3. **GDPR compliance** — as an Irish-registered company you are directly subject to GDPR, which is actually a selling point ("we are GDPR compliant by design")
4. **Investor access** — EU and US investors are more familiar with Irish company structures, and Enterprise Ireland only supports Irish-incorporated companies
5. **Banking** — Irish Stripe/Revolut business accounts are simpler for global payments
6. **Tax** — 12.5% Irish CT vs 30% Nigerian CIT on profits

### When to Use the Nigerian Atensai Entity

- Nigeria-specific apps or services (localised products for the Nigerian market)
- Local Nigerian contracts, partnerships, freelancers
- If you want to keep revenue from Nigerian clients inside Nigeria (to avoid withholding tax complexities on repatriation)
- Any products targeting Nigeria exclusively

> **Practical move:** The Nigerian Atensai entity can be a **distributor or reseller** of the Irish company's products in Nigeria, or simply a separate entity that you run independently. Don't merge the IP — keep it clean in Ireland.

## What to Do With the atensai.com Domain

The domain is the brand's anchor. Since the Irish entity will be the primary commercial entity:

1. **Transfer ownership of atensai.com to Atensai Limited (Ireland)** once incorporated
   - This is done via a domain transfer or change of registrant details
   - Include this in the IP Assignment Agreement

2. **Update the website** to reflect the Irish entity as the primary business
   - Add an Irish address and company registration number to the footer
   - Update privacy policy and terms to reference Irish law and GDPR

3. **Nigerian operations** can use a subdomain (ng.atensai.com) or a separate landing page to keep the Nigerian entity distinct

## Anouka Farms — Keep Completely Separate

### Why

- Different sector (agriculture vs. technology) with very different risk profiles
- Different investors, partners, and audiences
- Mixing the two could confuse brand, add complexity, and create tax complications
- Farming income in Nigeria is subject to different tax treatment

### What This Means Practically

- Never route Anouka Farms income or expenses through Atensai
- Keep separate accounts, separate records
- Do not mention Anouka Farms on Atensai's investor materials (unless directly relevant to an agri-tech pivot you intend)
- atensai.com and anoukafarms.com remain separate brands

## Nigeria-Ireland Tax Considerations

### Double Taxation Agreement (DTA)

Ireland and Nigeria have a **Double Taxation Agreement (DTA)** in force. This matters when:

- You pay yourself a salary from the Irish company while living in Ireland
- The Irish company earns revenue from Nigeria
- You eventually return to Nigeria or split your time between countries

Key DTA provisions:
- Generally, you are taxed in the country of residence for employment income
- Dividends from the Irish company paid to you (a Nigerian national) may be subject to Irish DWT + potentially Nigerian income tax on receipt — the DTA limits this double taxation

> Get specific advice from a tax advisor experienced in both Irish and Nigerian tax before you start paying dividends or repatriating significant funds.

### Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Payments

When the Irish company pays the Nigerian entity (e.g., for services, royalties, or management fees):
- Ireland may withhold tax at source on certain payment types
- Nigeria may impose withholding tax on inbound payments
- The DTA sets maximum rates that override domestic rates

### Transfer Pricing

If the Irish company and Nigerian Atensai are **related entities** (same owner), any transactions between them must be at **arm's length prices** — i.e., the same price you'd charge an unrelated third party. Revenue (Ireland) and FIRS (Nigeria) both monitor this. Keep records if you have inter-company transactions.

## Governance Recommendations

### Irish Company (Atensai Limited)
- **Director:** You (after Stamp 4)
- **Company Secretary:** You initially, or engage a company secretary service (~€200/year)
- **Registered Office:** Use a registered office service until you have a physical office

### Nigerian Atensai
- Keep the existing structure
- Ensure company is in good standing with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)
- Keep it active even if dormant — it's your backup and Nigeria-facing channel

### Anouka Farms
- Maintain completely independently
- Decide whether to expand, partner, or hold based on that business's own merits
