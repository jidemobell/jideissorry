## Step 1 — Before Stamp 4 (Now → Aug 2026)

Use this window to prepare everything so you can move fast the moment Stamp 4 arrives.

### Preparation Checklist

- [ ] **Reserve your company name** — check availability on the CRO (Companies Registration Office) website: [cro.ie](https://www.cro.ie). Preferred name: **Atensai Limited** or **Atensai Technologies Limited**.
- [ ] **Choose your company type** — almost certainly a **Private Company Limited by Shares (LTD)**. It is the simplest, most founder-friendly structure in Ireland.
- [ ] **Identify a registered office address** — must be a physical Irish address. Options: use a registered office service (~€100–300/year) until you have your own office.
- [ ] **Identify a company secretary** — every Irish company needs one. Can be yourself if you are a director, or you can appoint a service provider.
- [ ] **Draft a business description (Memorandum & Articles)** — for a tech startup, use a broad "any lawful trade" clause so you are not restricted later.
- [ ] **Open a personal Irish bank account** if not done. Required before a business account is possible.
- [ ] **Get a PPSN** if you don't already have one.

## Step 2 — Immediately After Stamp 4 Is Granted

### Register the Company (CRO)

**Method 1 — DIY (cheapest):**
- Use the CRO online portal [core.ie](https://core.ie)
- File Form A1 (registration form) + Constitution
- Fee: **€50** (online)
- Processing: 3–5 working days

**Method 2 — Use a company formation agent (recommended for first-time founders):**
- Cost: **€100–300 all-in** (they handle A1, registered office, company seal)
- Example providers: Company Bureau, Formations Direct, CROdirect
- Turnaround: 1–3 business days

### What Gets Created

After registration you receive:
1. **Certificate of Incorporation** (your company's birth certificate)
2. **Company Registration Number (CRN)** — quote this everywhere
3. **The company is now a legal entity**

## Step 3 — After Incorporation

### Revenue Registration (Tax)

Register with **Revenue Commissioners** (revenue.ie) within the first few months:
- Apply for a **Tax Registration Number (TRN)** for the company
- Register for **Corporation Tax (CT)**
- Register for **VAT** (optional if revenue < €37,500/year for services, but register early if you expect to scale or deal with EU clients)
- Register for **Employer PRSI/PAYE** only when you start paying yourself or employees

Use Revenue's myAccount / ROS (Revenue Online Service) system online.

### Business Bank Account

Open a business bank account. In Ireland, options for startups:
- **AIB / Bank of Ireland / Ulster Bank** — traditional, slow to onboard
- **Revolut Business** — fast, digital-first, good for startups, free tier available
- **Wise Business** — excellent for cross-border payments (relevant for Nigerian business links)
- **Starling Bank Business** — UK-based but works for some Irish structures

> Recommendation: **Revolut Business** to start (fast, instant IBAN, multi-currency), then add a traditional bank account when needed for grants/funding.

## Step 4 — Directors and Shareholding

### Directors

Every Irish LTD needs **at least one director** who is resident in the EEA, **OR** must post a **Section 137 bond** (~€25,000 surety bond from an insurance company) if no EEA director.

Once you have **Stamp 4**, you are treated as ordinarily resident in Ireland — you **qualify as the EEA-resident director**. No bond needed.

### Shareholding from Day One

| Shareholder | Shares | % |
|-------------|--------|---|
| You (founder) | 1,000 | 100% |

You can issue additional shares later to co-founders, employees (via share option schemes), or investors. Keep 100% until you have a clear reason to dilute.

## Important Warning

> ⚠️ **Do NOT register an Irish company while on Stamp 1.** Doing so and actively operating it (receiving revenue, signing as director, making commercial decisions) could be considered self-employment and violates your Stamp 1 conditions. Wait for the Stamp 4 letter before filing with CRO.
