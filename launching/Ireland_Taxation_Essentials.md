# Ireland Taxation Essentials for a Tech Founder

**Last Updated:** March 29, 2026  
**Context:** Nigerian founder, transitioning to Stamp 4, incorporating Atensai Limited in Ireland

> ⚠️ This document is for orientation and planning purposes. Always consult a qualified Irish tax advisor (tax consultant or chartered accountant) for your specific circumstances before making financial decisions.

---

## The Big Picture: Why Ireland Is Good for Tech

Ireland is one of the most startup-friendly tax jurisdictions in the world:
- **12.5% Corporation Tax** on trading income (one of the lowest in the OECD)
- **Knowledge Development Box** (6.25% effective rate on IP-derived income)
- **R&D Tax Credit** (25% credit on qualifying expenditure)
- **Entrepreneur Relief** (10% CGT on company exit, up to €1M lifetime)
- EU membership + English language = access to the EU market
- Strong ecosystem (Google, Meta, Apple, LinkedIn all have EMEA HQs in Dublin)

## Key Tax Concepts — The Essential Vocabulary

### Corporation Tax (CT)

The tax your **company** pays on its profits.

| Type of Income | Rate |
|---------------|------|
| Trading income (active business) | **12.5%** |
| Non-trading income (passive: rent, dividends, royalties) | **25%** |
| Foreign income (certain circumstances) | **25%** |

**Key rules:**
- Taxable on profits, not revenue (revenue minus allowable expenses)
- Returns filed annually with Revenue (ROS platform)
- Preliminary tax must be paid during the year (not just at year end)
- Small companies: 6 months + 1 day after year-end for returns

**Global Minimum Tax (Pillar Two):** As of 2024, companies with >€750M global revenue must pay a 15% minimum rate. This does **not** affect a startup — the 12.5% rate still applies to you until you're very large.

---

### VAT (Value Added Tax)

Like sales tax but applied throughout the supply chain.

| Rate | Category |
|------|----------|
| **23%** | Standard (most digital services, B2C) |
| **13.5%** | Reduced (some hospitality, property) |
| **9%** | Tourism, newspapers |
| **0%** | Books, children's clothing, some food |

**For app developers specifically:**
- Your app subscription revenue to Irish/EU consumers → likely **23% VAT**
- **VAT registration threshold:** €37,500/year for services — once you cross this, you *must* register for VAT
- **Register early anyway** — it allows you to reclaim VAT on purchases (software subscriptions, hardware, office expenses, etc.)
- **EU Digital Services rules:** If you sell digital services to EU consumers in other countries, you may owe VAT in their countries. Use the **OSS (One Stop Shop)** scheme via Revenue — file one VAT return in Ireland for all EU sales.

**B2B sales (business-to-business):** Usually VAT is handled by the customer via "reverse charge" — you don't charge them VAT if they give you their VAT number.

---

### Income Tax (IT)

This is **your personal tax** as an individual.

| Income Band | Rate |
|-------------|------|
| Up to €42,000 (single) | **20%** |
| Above €42,000 | **40%** |

**Allowances:**
- Personal Tax Credit: €1,875
- Employee Tax Credit: €1,875 (if employed)
- Home Carer, Medical, etc.

Once you have a company, you will typically **draw a salary** from the company, which is taxed as income. You also need to consider whether to take **dividends** (which have their own tax treatment).

---

### USC (Universal Social Charge)

Applies to all income above €13,000/year.

| Income | Rate |
|--------|------|
| €0–€12,012 | 0.5% |
| €12,012–€25,760 | 2% |
| €25,760–€70,044 | 4% |
| Above €70,044 | 8% |

> USC applies on top of Income Tax. So at high salary levels you could be paying 40% IT + 8% USC = **48%** marginal rate on personal income. This is why founders often balance salary + dividends to minimise personal tax.

---

### PRSI (Pay Related Social Insurance)

Contributes to your social welfare entitlements (state pension, illness benefit, etc.)

| Role | Rate |
|------|------|
| Employee (Class A) | **4%** on all income |
| Employer | **11.05%** on gross wage bill |
| Self-employed (Class S) | **4%** (minimum €500/year) |

> When you pay yourself a salary from your company, the company pays employer PRSI (11.05%) on your salary. Factor this into payroll costs.

---

### CGT (Capital Gains Tax)

Tax on profits when you **sell** an asset (shares, property, IP, etc.)

- Standard rate: **33%**
- Annual exemption: **€1,270** per person

**Entrepreneur Relief:** If you sell your company (or shares in it) and you meet certain criteria (founder, held ≥5% for ≥3 years, director/employee), you pay only **10% CGT** on the first **€1,000,000 cumulative lifetime gains**. This is a very significant incentive for founders.

---

### R&D Tax Credit

**Very important for app development businesses.**

- You can claim a **25% tax credit** on qualifying R&D expenditure
- This is a credit against your Corporation Tax bill (not just a deduction)
- If you spend €100,000 on R&D and owe €12,500 in CT, the R&D credit of €25,000 would wipe out the CT and generate a **€12,500 cash refund from Revenue**
- Qualifying costs: salaries of developers doing R&D activities, cloud computing for R&D, equipment, subcontractors doing R&D work

> Most app development that involves innovation (novel algorithms, new approaches) can qualify as R&D. Keep records of the time spent and nature of the work.

---

### Knowledge Development Box (KDB)

If your business earns income from **qualifying IP** you developed yourself (patents, copyright software), that income can be taxed at an effective rate of **6.25% instead of 12.5%**.

This applies to income from:
- Qualifying patents
- Copyright software you created
- Certain other IP assets

> Very relevant for your dating app, if you develop the algorithm in-house and patent it. The income from licensing or operating that patented technology could qualify for the KDB rate.

### Key Annual Filings for an Irish Company

| Filing | Deadline | Filed With |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Annual Return (B1) | Within 56 days of ARD | CRO |
| Corporation Tax Return | 6 months + 1 day after year end | Revenue (ROS) |
| VAT Returns | Bi-monthly or 6-monthly | Revenue |
| PAYE/Employer Returns | Monthly | Revenue |
| Personal Tax Return (Form 11) | October 31 each year | Revenue |

> **Getting an accountant is essentially mandatory.** A good startup accountant in Ireland costs ~€1,500–4,000/year and saves you far more in time and avoided penalties. Look for one familiar with tech startups and Revenue digital services rules.

---

### Start-Up Relief (Section 486C)

For newly incorporated Irish companies:
- **Corporation tax relief for the first 3 years** of trading
- Relief is linked to the amount of employer PRSI you pay (capped at €5,000 PRSI per employee)
- Effectively: if you pay yourself a salary and hire employees, you can reduce/eliminate your CT bill in early years
- Maximum relief: **€40,000 per year**

> Small but useful in early stage — ask your accountant to apply this automatically.

## Salary vs Dividends: The Founder Dilemma

Once your company has profits, you have a choice in how to extract money:

### Option 1: Salary
- Deductible expense for the company (reduces CT)
- Taxed at income tax rates (20/40%) + USC + PRSI
- Builds PRSI record (social welfare entitlements, pension)

### Option 2: Dividends
- Paid from after-tax company profits (CT already paid)
- Taxed personally at **25% Dividend Withholding Tax (DWT)** at source, offset against final income tax liability
- No PRSI on dividends
- Does not reduce company profit for CT purposes

### Typical Founder Strategy
- Pay yourself a **moderate salary** (~€50,000–70,000) to use the 20% band and build PRSI contributions
- Take additional profits as **dividends** if the company is profitable
- Always run this past an accountant — the optimal mix changes with your income level

## Immediate Action (Before Company Exists)

- [ ] Get a **PPSN** (PPS Number) if you don't already have one — needed for everything tax-related in Ireland
- [ ] Open **myAccount** on revenue.ie with your PPSN
- [ ] Research accountants who specialise in startups (ask at LEO, Enterprise Ireland events, or Dogpatch Labs Dublin)
- [ ] Attend a **Local Enterprise Office** free startup workshop — they cover LEO grants and tax basics
